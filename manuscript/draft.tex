\title{Neural-Network Variational Monte Carlo for the 2D
       Cavity-QED Electron Gas:\\
       Lang--Firsov-Aware Ansatz and Twist-Averaged Finite-Size
       Correction}

\author{C.~W.~Myung}
\affiliation{\todo{affiliations}}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We present a neural-network variational Monte Carlo (NN-VMC)
treatment of the two-dimensional homogeneous electron gas (2D HEG)
strongly coupled to a single cavity mode in the velocity (P) gauge of
the Pauli--Fierz Hamiltonian.  The trial wavefunction combines a
FermiNet-style permutation-equivariant orbital backbone with a plane-wave
envelope, a periodic Kato cusp, the Smith deep Jastrow~\cite{Smith2024},
a coordinate backflow, and two Lang--Firsov-aware photon-conditioning
modules: a real coherent shift $Q_0(\mathbf{R})$ and an explicit
$P_0(\mathbf{R}) q_c$ phase coupling.  Total parameter count is
$\sim$170k.  We validate the architecture against the analytic
electron--photon correlation functional of Weber \emph{et al.}~%
\cite{Weber2024} across a coupling scan
$\gamma \in \{0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 0.90\}$.  Using independently
trained $\Gamma$-only V11\_on and V11\_off chkpts at $N=18$, the
cavity-induced electron--photon correlation correction is
$\Ehp(\Gamma; N{=}18, \gamma{=}0.5) = -0.175 \pm 0.021$~mHa/e,
$\sim$25\% less negative than Weber's $N{=}\infty$ analytic value
$-0.236$~mHa/e --- consistent with the expected $O(1/N)$ finite-size
deficit.  We additionally implement a twist-averaged boundary-condition
(TABC) evaluation pipeline based on a closed-form
$\cos/\sin \to e^{i \mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}}$ coefficient transfer
and a per-twist warm-start fine-tune.  The TABC results expose a
subtle asymmetric trial-relaxation issue: matter-conditioned
Lang--Firsov MLPs trained at $\Gamma$ generate $|\bm\kappa|$-dependent
biases that do \emph{not} cancel between V11\_on and V11\_off.  We
conclude that for the cavity correlation observable $\Ehp$ --- where
the bare-HEG kinetic energy cancels exactly in the ON--OFF
subtraction --- $\Gamma$-only at closed-shell N is both the cleanest
and the most accurate comparison to analytic theory; TABC is only
needed for absolute thermodynamic-limit estimates.  Closed-shell N
ladder convergence to Weber's $N{=}\infty$ value is identified as
the natural next milestone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================
Strong light--matter coupling in optical cavities has emerged as a
versatile platform for reshaping the equilibrium phase diagram of
correlated electron matter
~\cite{Hubener2021,Schlawin2022,Garcia-Vidal2021}.  The Pauli--Fierz
Hamiltonian in the long-wavelength limit captures the essential
non-perturbative physics for a single cavity mode at frequency $\Omega$
and dimensionless coupling $\lambda$, mixing photonic and electronic
degrees of freedom through the displacement
$\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r}) = \lambda \, \hat{\bm{\epsilon}}\, q_c$
(velocity gauge).  Recent quantum-Monte-Carlo work by Weber
\emph{et al.}~\cite{Weber2024} mapped out the cavity-induced
electron--electron correlation across a wide coupling range in the
2D HEG via auxiliary-field QMC plus a closed-form correlation
functional [their Eq.~(6)].

Neural-network variational Monte Carlo (NN-VMC), with FermiNet
\cite{Pfau2020}, PauliNet \cite{Hermann2020}, PsiFormer
\cite{vonGlehn2023}, and Smith \emph{et al.}'s deep Jastrow
\cite{Smith2024} as representative ans\"atze, has reached high
accuracy on molecular and bulk-matter problems with significantly
fewer determinants than traditional Slater--Jastrow trials.  Extending
the formalism to coupled electron--photon systems requires (i) a
joint $(\mathbf{r}, q_c)$ ansatz with a non-factorisable photon
amplitude and phase, (ii) Lang--Firsov-aware coherent shifts that
absorb the matter-driven mean-field photon displacement, and
(iii) finite-size handling via twisted boundary conditions
\cite{Lin2001,Drummond2008} when comparing to thermodynamic-limit
references.

In this work we develop and validate such an ansatz, the ``V11''
architecture, for the cavity-coupled 2D HEG at the Weber
\emph{et al.}~Fig.~1(b) operating point (no Coulomb interaction; weak
cosine external potential $V_{\mathrm{ext}}(\mathbf{r}) = -A
\sum_d \cos(2\pi r_d / a)$; $N=18$ electrons in a square cell;
$\Omega = 0.1$~Ha).  Our key contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item A self-contained NN-VMC architecture for 2D cavity-QED HEG
      (Sec.~\ref{sec:ansatz}), with $\sim$170k trainable parameters,
      that ties the photon Gaussian width and centre to electron-only
      features through learnable $Q_0(\mathbf{R})$, $P_0(\mathbf{R})$,
      and $S(\mathbf{R})$ MLPs.  This embeds the Lang--Firsov
      mean-field channel structurally rather than asking the bulk
      phase MLP to discover it.
\item A closed-form coefficient transfer $\cos/\sin \to e^{i \mathbf{k}
      \cdot \mathbf{r}}$ from the real $\Gamma$-point envelope to the
      complex twisted envelope (Sec.~\ref{sec:tabc}).  The transfer
      is exact up to a partial-shell boundary at the Fermi edge,
      verified by a $\bm{\kappa}{=}0$ regression test matching the
      $\Gamma$-only trained energy to within Monte-Carlo noise.
\item A pivot from $\bm{\kappa}$-aware NN architecture to
      \emph{per-twist warm-start fine-tuning} (Sec.~\ref{sec:perk}):
      each Halton-sequence twist starts from the same $\Gamma$-trained
      chkpt and undergoes a short SR-VMC relaxation against its own
      twisted basis, giving the variationally-best TABC energy
      without architectural changes.
\item A complete L5 stochastic-reconfiguration pipeline modification
      (Sec.~\ref{sec:complex-sr}) that handles \emph{complex-valued}
      orbital coefficients by splitting each complex parameter into
      its $(\mathrm{Re}, \mathrm{Im})$ pair at the SR/parameter
      boundary, leaving all downstream momentum, clipping, and
      linear-solve algebra real.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows.
Sec.~\ref{sec:hamiltonian} states the Pauli--Fierz Hamiltonian and
the operating point.  Sec.~\ref{sec:ansatz} presents the V11 ansatz
in full.  Sec.~\ref{sec:optimizer} describes the SR + SPRING
optimiser.  Sec.~\ref{sec:tabc} develops the TABC pipeline.
Sec.~\ref{sec:results} reports validation results, comparison to
Weber \emph{et al.}'s analytic functional, and preliminary per-twist
fine-tune outcomes.  Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion} closes.

\section{System and Hamiltonian}\label{sec:hamiltonian}
% =====================================================================
We consider $N$ spin-$\tfrac12$ electrons in a square 2D cell of side
$L$ with periodic boundary conditions, coupled to a single transverse
cavity mode of frequency $\Omega$, polarisation
$\hat{\bm\epsilon} \in \mathbb{R}^2$, and dimensionless coupling
$\lambda$.  In the long-wavelength velocity gauge the Pauli--Fierz
Hamiltonian reads
\begin{align}
\hat H &= \hat T_e + \hat V_{ee} + \hat V_{\mathrm{ext}}
       + \hat H_{\mathrm{ph}}
       + \lambda \hat{q}_c \, \hat{\bm\epsilon} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{P}}
       + \tfrac12 N \lambda^2,\\
\hat T_e &= -\tfrac12 \sum_i \nabla_i^2,\quad
\hat H_{\mathrm{ph}} = \tfrac12 (\hat p_c^2 + \Omega^2 \hat q_c^2),
\end{align}
where $\hat{\mathbf P} = -i\sum_i \nabla_i$ is the total electronic
momentum and the $N\lambda^2/2$ diamagnetic term enforces gauge
invariance.  For the present study we set
$\hat V_{ee} \equiv 0$ (non-interacting matter, matching the Weber
operating point), retain a weak periodic
$\hat V_{\mathrm{ext}}(\mathbf{r}) = -A \sum_{d} \cos(2\pi r_d / a)$
with $A = 0.3$~Ha and $a = 3$~Bohr to break translation
invariance.  Throughout we use $N=18$ (closed shell in 2D),
$r_s = 1.5958$ (so $L = r_s\sqrt{\pi N} \simeq 12$~Bohr), $\Omega
= 0.1$~Ha.

A convenient dimensionless coupling is the photon-displacement
weight in the dressed photon frequency:
\begin{equation}
\Oeff = \sqrt{\Omega^2 + N \lambda^2},\quad
\gamma = \frac{N \lambda^2}{\Omega^2 + N \lambda^2}.
\end{equation}
Throughout we present results across
$\gamma \in \{0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 0.90\}$.

\section{V11 Trial Wavefunction}\label{sec:ansatz}
% =====================================================================
We adopt the joint matter--photon ansatz
\begin{equation}
\Psi(\mathbf{R}, q_c) = \psi_e(\mathbf{R}) \cdot \chi_{\Oeff}(q_c)
                       \cdot \exp(u(\mathbf{R}, q_c)
                       + i \varphi(\mathbf{R}, q_c)),
\label{eq:ansatz}
\end{equation}
factorised into a real-valued electronic Slater--Jastrow trial
$\psi_e$, a photon Gaussian $\chi_{\Oeff}$, and a complex coupling
$u + i\varphi$ between the two subsystems.

\subsection{Electronic block $\psi_e$}
The matter trunk has five layers, totalling $\sim$115k parameters
(out of 168k):

\begin{description}
\item[GNN backbone] A FermiNet message-passing network
      \cite{Pfau2020} with embedding dimension 64, three interaction
      layers and a 16-dimensional two-particle stream.  Produces
      per-electron features $h_i^{(T)}$.
\item[Plane-wave envelope] At the $\Gamma$ point each orbital
      $\phi_a^{(d)}(\mathbf{r})$ is a real linear combination of
      cosines and sines on the closed-shell PW basis:
      \begin{equation}
      \phi_a^{(d)}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_n c^{\cos,(d)}_{a,n}\cos(\mathbf k_n\cdot \mathbf r)
                              + c^{\sin,(d)}_{a,n}\sin(\mathbf k_n\cdot \mathbf r),
      \end{equation}
      with $n$ indexing the lowest 18 cos/sin entries.  For the TABC
      variant the envelope is built on the twisted complex basis
      $\{e^{i (\mathbf k_n + \bm\kappa)\cdot \mathbf r}\}$
      (Sec.~\ref{sec:tabc}).
\item[Full-determinant slicing] The Slater matrix is constructed
      from the full $(\text{up} \oplus \text{down})$ orbital block, so
      both spins see all 18 orbital columns
      (\texttt{full\_determinant} = True).  This is variationally
      stronger than the block-diagonal Slater of standard
      spin-restricted ans\"atze.
\item[Coordinate backflow] $\mathbf{r}_i \to \mathbf{r}_i + W_{bf}
      h_i^{(T)}$~\cite{Smith2024}.  Used by both the envelope and
      the Smith deep Jastrow but not by the cusp or pair Jastrow
      (those depend on physical electron coordinates).
\item[Smith deep Jastrow] \cite{Smith2024} The MLP scalar
      $J_{\rm DJ}(h_i^{(T)}, \mathrm{Linear}_{\rm pre}(\mathbf{r}_i))$
      summed over electrons.  Replaces the simple GNN-side Jastrow
      (mutually exclusive at the builder level).
\item[Periodic Kato cusp] Standard 2D Kato cusp
      $u(r) = -\mathrm{scale} \cdot \alpha^2/(\alpha + r)$ with
      a smooth $\tfrac12(1 + \cos(\pi r/r_{\rm cut}))$
      cell-face cutoff at $r_{\rm cut} = L/2$.
      $\alpha$ is softplus-parameterised to keep it strictly
      positive.
\end{description}

\subsection{Photon block $\chi_{\Oeff}(q_c)$}
The photon amplitude is a single Gaussian of width $1/\sqrt{S}$
centred at the matter-conditioned coherent shift $Q_0(\mathbf{R})$:
\begin{equation}
\log \chi_{\Oeff}(q_c|\mathbf{R}) =
  -\tfrac12 S(\mathbf{R})(q_c - Q_0(\mathbf{R}))^2
  + \tfrac12 \log |S(\mathbf{R})| - \tfrac12 \log \pi.
\label{eq:chi}
\end{equation}
At iter~0 all of $Q_0$, $\log(S/\Oeff)$ are MLP outputs initialised
to zero (zero-init last layer), so the photon block reduces to the
bare harmonic-oscillator ground state of frequency $\Oeff$.

\subsection{Lang--Firsov-aware coupling MLPs}\label{sec:lf-mlps}
For coupling operator $\hat O = \hat{\bm\epsilon}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{P}}$
(velocity gauge), the lowest-order Lang--Firsov transform predicts
a coherent shift in the photon momentum $\hat p_c$ proportional to
$\hat{\bm\epsilon}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{\rm CoM}$ \cite{Schaefer2018}.  We
encode this structurally with an explicit phase contribution
\begin{equation}
\varphi(\mathbf{R}, q_c) \supset P_0(\mathbf{R})\, q_c,
\end{equation}
where $P_0$ is a small MLP whose features include the matter
centre-of-mass projected onto the cavity polarisation,
$\hat{\bm\epsilon}\cdot \mathbf{R}_{\rm CoM}$, in addition to a
truncated Fourier-mode collective representation
$\{F^{\rm sin}_{\mathbf{K}}, F^{\rm cos}_{\mathbf{K}}\}$ for
$|\mathbf{K}| \le K_{\max}$ ($K_{\max} = 6$, giving 112 modes per
spin).  Without this explicit channel the
$\hat\epsilon \cdot \mathbf{P}$ coupling is absorbed implicitly into
$\varphi_{\rm MLP}$ at a substantially higher variance cost.

The complete coupling is then
\begin{align}
u(\mathbf{R}, q_c) &= \mathrm{MLP}_{\rm mag}(F(\mathbf{R}), q_c), \\
\varphi(\mathbf{R}, q_c) &= \mathrm{MLP}_{\rm phase}(F(\mathbf{R}), q_c)
                          + P_0(\mathbf{R})\, q_c.
\end{align}
Both $u$ and $\varphi$ MLPs have two hidden layers of width 64 and
zero-init last layers.

\subsection{Parameter count}
At our operating point the V11 ansatz totals 168{,}468
parameters: $\sim$115k in the matter trunk, $\sim$19k in each of the
$u$ and $\varphi$ MLPs, plus $\sim$8k in $Q_0$, $P_0$, and $S$
combined.

\section{Optimiser and Local Energy}\label{sec:optimizer}
% =====================================================================
We train via stochastic reconfiguration (SR) in the dual SMW form
\cite{Park2024}: at each iteration we form the empirical Fisher
matrix $\hat S = \langle J^\dagger J\rangle / n_w$ from the per-walker
parameter Jacobians $J_{u,v} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_w \times n_p}$ of the
real/imaginary log~$\Psi$, then solve
$(\hat S + \lambda I) \bm{\delta\theta} = \mathbf{g}$ using the
Sherman--Morrison--Woodbury identity to reduce the linear system to
a $2n_w \times 2n_w$ walker-space solve (with $\lambda = 10^{-3}$
damping).  The full procedure is wrapped in a single fused JIT
\texttt{train\_step} closure that contains $n_{\rm decorr} = 15$
walker decorrelation steps, the local-energy evaluation, the Jacobian
batched-vmap, and the SR update inline.

The local energy follows the standard complex-$\Psi$ formula with
real Coulomb (Ewald, here off), real $V_{\rm ext}$, a velocity-gauge
coupling
$\langle \hat O \rangle_{\rm loc} = -i \hat{\bm\epsilon}\cdot
\nabla_{\rm R} \log \Psi$, and an exact one-mode photon
Hamiltonian.

For the long-fine-tune runs that use SPRING momentum ($\mu=0.9$)
\cite{Goldshlager2024} and F-norm clipping ($c_{\rm clip} = 0.1$),
$\Gamma$-point V11 converges to a Monte-Carlo standard error of
order $10^{-5}$~Ha/e per block in $\sim$30{,}000 SR iterations on a
single GH200 GPU (wall: $\sim$3~hr).

\section{Twist-Averaged Boundary Conditions}\label{sec:tabc}
% =====================================================================
\subsection{Formalism}
For a finite cell of side $L$ with twist
$\bm\kappa\in [-\tfrac12, \tfrac12]^2$, the single-particle plane
waves are $e^{i(\mathbf k_n + \bm\kappa) \cdot \mathbf{r}}$ with
$\mathbf{k}_n = (2\pi/L)\,\mathbf{n}$ for $\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^2$.
The TABC energy is
\begin{equation}
E_{\rm TABC} = \int_{\rm BZ} d^2\kappa \;
               \langle \hat H \rangle_{\bm\kappa}
             \approx \frac{1}{n_\kappa}
               \sum_{h=1}^{n_\kappa}
               \langle \hat H\rangle_{\bm\kappa_h}.
\label{eq:tabc-def}
\end{equation}
Following Weber \emph{et al.}~\cite{Weber2024} we sample
$\bm\kappa_h$ from a 60-point Halton low-discrepancy sequence
\cite{Halton1960} shifted to $[-\tfrac12, \tfrac12]^2$, skipping the
$(0,0)$ entry.

\subsection{$\cos/\sin \to e^{i\mathbf k \cdot \mathbf r}$ transfer}
The V11 envelope is trained at $\Gamma$ on a basis of cosines and
sines on the lowest 18 $|{\mathbf k}|^2$ cell vectors.  For TABC eval
at twist $\bm\kappa$ we need the equivalent complex coefficients on
the twisted basis $\{e^{i (\mathbf k_n + \bm\kappa)\cdot \mathbf{r}}\}$.
The conversion is exact, via the identities
\begin{align}
\cos(\mathbf{k}\cdot \mathbf{r}) &= \tfrac12 \left(
   e^{+i\mathbf k\cdot \mathbf r} + e^{-i\mathbf k\cdot \mathbf r}\right),\\
\sin(\mathbf{k}\cdot \mathbf{r}) &= \tfrac{1}{2i}\left(
   e^{+i\mathbf k\cdot \mathbf r} - e^{-i\mathbf k\cdot \mathbf r}\right).
\end{align}
For a real-basis orbital
$\phi(\mathbf r) = c^{\cos}_n \cos(\mathbf k_n\cdot \mathbf r) + c^{\sin}_n
\sin(\mathbf k_n\cdot \mathbf r)$ (one $n$ at a time):
\begin{equation}
\phi(\mathbf r) =
   \tfrac{c^{\cos}_n - i c^{\sin}_n}{2}\, e^{+i\mathbf k_n\cdot \mathbf r}
 + \tfrac{c^{\cos}_n + i c^{\sin}_n}{2}\, e^{-i\mathbf k_n\cdot \mathbf r}.
\label{eq:transfer}
\end{equation}
At the $\bm\kappa = 0$ limit Eq.~\eqref{eq:transfer} reproduces V11's
trained $\psi_e$ exactly (verified up to MC noise; see
Sec.~\ref{sec:results-kzero}).  At $\bm\kappa \neq 0$ the
\emph{coefficients} are unchanged; only the basis functions shift to
$e^{i(\mathbf k_n + \bm\kappa)\cdot \mathbf{r}}$, i.e., we apply the
Bloch ansatz with the unaltered periodic part.  Identification
between the real and complex bases is by integer $\mathbf{n}$-vector,
so the transfer is twist-agnostic at the matching level.

Three technical subtleties must be respected for the transfer to be
faithful:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Antipodal closure.} The real basis stores one
      $\mathbf{n}$-vector per $\pm$-pair, while the complex basis
      stores both.  We bump the complex envelope's
      \texttt{init\_pw\_count} to the next complete shell
      ($N_{\rm PW} = 21$ at our $N = 18$ closed shell) so that every
      real $\pm\mathbf{k}_n$ partner is represented in the complex
      basis.  Without this, the partial-shell at $|{\mathbf k}|^2 = 5$
      drops $\sim 8$~mHa/e of orbital weight (smaller basis $\to$
      higher variational E).
\item \textbf{\texttt{basis\_is\_sin}-aware activity.} For the
      Fermi-edge shell some sine entries are absent from the
      $\Gamma$-trained basis; their coefficient slots store
      uninitialised random init noise.  We track per-$\mathbf{n}$
      \texttt{active\_cos} / \texttt{active\_sin} flags from the real
      basis's \texttt{basis\_is\_sin} index and only transfer
      activated cosines/sines.
\item \textbf{\texttt{full\_determinant} support in the complex
      envelope.}  The complex envelope's original
      \texttt{full\_determinant=True} path was implicitly
      block-diagonal (up electrons only evaluated on up orbitals,
      down on down).  This breaks the equivalence to the real
      envelope's true full-det semantics (every electron on every
      orbital).  We add a non-block-diagonal path that matches the
      real envelope at $\bm\kappa = 0$.
\item \textbf{Smith deep Jastrow invocation.} The complex wf
      stored but never called \texttt{self.smith\_deep\_jastrow}.
      We fix the call site, recovering the missing $\sim$48~mHa/e
      at $\bm\kappa = 0$ on V11 chkpts.
\end{enumerate}

After all four fixes the $\bm\kappa = 0$ regression test passes
(Sec.~\ref{sec:results-kzero}).

\subsection{Per-twist warm-start fine-tune}\label{sec:perk}
The transfer Eq.~\eqref{eq:transfer} provides an excellent
\emph{starting point} at each $\bm\kappa$ but a \emph{biased}
energy estimate: V11's orbital coefficients are optimised for
$\bm\kappa = 0$ and become sub-optimal at twisted boundary
conditions.  This is the standard "trial-overfit at $\Gamma$"
problem~\cite{Krogel2013}.

Two well-known fixes exist in NN-VMC: (a) train a single,
$\bm\kappa$-conditioned trial wavefunction (we implement and document
a $\bm\kappa$-aware variant in Sec.~\ref{sec:complex-sr}); or (b)
\emph{per-twist} train a separate trial wavefunction.  Option (b)
gives the variationally-best TABC bound for any given ansatz family,
since the only constraint is that each $\bm\kappa$ independently
minimises within the same family.  We adopt (b) with warm-start: at
each Halton twist $\bm\kappa_h$ we
\begin{enumerate}
\item build a fresh $\bm\kappa_h$-twisted optimiser with the V11
      architecture,
\item populate its envelope coefficients from the $\Gamma$-trained
      V11 chkpt via Eq.~\eqref{eq:transfer},
\item run $K$ SR-VMC iterations at $\bm\kappa_h$ (we use $K=10^4$
      with $\mathrm{lr} = 5\times10^{-3}$, $n_w = 1024$),
\item save the per-twist chkpt and final $\langle\hat
      H\rangle_{\bm\kappa_h}$.
\end{enumerate}
The TABC average $E_{\rm TABC} = n_\kappa^{-1}\sum_h E_h$ is in
principle unbiased modulo the per-twist Monte-Carlo standard error.
In practice, however, the limited number of SR iterations per twist
($K=10^4$) and the small learning rate inherited from V11 training
($\mathrm{lr} = 5\times10^{-3}$, cosine-decayed) leave large-$|\bm\kappa|$
twists incompletely relaxed, with the per-twist energy still strongly
$|\bm\kappa|$-correlated even after fine-tune (see
Sec.~\ref{sec:results-perk}).  This trial-relaxation residual is the
dominant difficulty in extending NN-VMC TABC beyond Jastrow-only
trials.

\subsection{Complex-parameter SR}\label{sec:complex-sr}
SR is conventionally derived for real parameters; the natural
gradient
$\bm{\delta\theta} = -\lambda^{-1}\hat S^{-1}\mathbf{g}$ involves
the empirical Fisher matrix, which for real $\theta$ is symmetric
positive semi-definite by construction.  When the orbital
coefficients $c \in \mathbb{C}$ are complex, both
$\nabla_c \log\Psi$ and the SR update direction become complex,
breaking the existing implementation that uses
$\mathtt{float()}$ projections of $\|g\|$ for convergence
diagnostics.

Rather than refactor the full SR algebra for Wirtinger derivatives,
we implement a \emph{real-doubled parameter representation} at the
SR boundary.  A custom \texttt{flatten\_to\_real} walks the
trial-wf pytree leaf by leaf: real leaves emit their values
unchanged, complex leaves emit
$[\mathrm{Re}(c), \mathrm{Im}(c)]$ as two real arrays of the same
shape.  The inverse \texttt{unravel\_real} reassembles them.  All
SR primitives (Jacobian vmap, F-norm clip, SMW solve, parameter
update) now see a real flat vector of dimension
$n_{\rm real} + 2 n_{\rm complex}$ and require no further
modification.  At $\bm\kappa = 0$ with zero-init $\bm\kappa$-MLPs
(see below), the doubled representation is bit-for-bit equivalent
to the standard real representation.

\subsection{$\bm\kappa$-aware variant (Phase 1)}
For completeness we sketch a $\bm\kappa$-conditioned architecture
in which $\bm\kappa$ becomes a runtime input to the wavefunction:
the envelope re-builds its $\{\mathbf{k}_n + \bm\kappa\}$ on the
fly, and a small MLP outputs a $\bm\kappa$-dependent additive
correction
$\Delta_a^{(d)}(\bm\kappa) = \mathrm{MLP}_\Delta(\bm\kappa)$ on
top of the stored base coefficients.  The zero-init last layer of
the $\Delta$-MLP makes $\Delta(\bm\kappa\equiv 0) \approx 0$, so
the architecture is bit-compatible with V11 at the $\Gamma$ point.
Training with random $\bm\kappa$ per step would relax the
$\bm\kappa$-overfit penalty without per-twist retraining, but
requires plumbing $\bm\kappa$ as a runtime input through the
fused SR primitive — a substantial surgery on the L5 driver and
deferred to future work.  Per-twist warm-start fine-tune
(Sec.~\ref{sec:perk}) achieves the same variational bound at the
cost of $n_\kappa$ separate SR runs but with no architectural
change required.

\subsection{$\bm\kappa{=}0$ regression}\label{sec:results-kzero}
The closed-form coefficient transfer Eq.~\eqref{eq:transfer},
combined with the four technical fixes enumerated in
Sec.~\ref{sec:tabc}, was validated by a $\bm\kappa = 0$ regression
test.  Loading the V11 $\gamma = 0.5$ chkpt
(168{,}468~params, $E_\Gamma = +137.10$~mHa/e), transferring its
$\cos/\sin$ coefficients to a freshly-built complex envelope at
$\bm\kappa = 0$, and re-evaluating gives
\begin{equation*}
E_{\bm\kappa = 0} = +137.058\pm 0.051~\text{mHa/e},
\qquad
\mathrm{Im}\langle E\rangle/N = -3.6\times 10^{-3}~\text{mHa/e},
\end{equation*}
in agreement with $E_\Gamma$ within MC noise.  Hermiticity holds to
$<10^{-4}$~mHa/e.

\subsection{Naive 60-twist TABC: trial-overfit penalty}\label{sec:results-tabc-naive}
We ran the 60-Halton TABC on the V11 $\gamma = 0.5$ chkpt using only
the coefficient transfer (\emph{no} per-twist fine-tune):

\begin{equation}
E_{\rm TABC} = +160.25 \pm 1.78~\text{mHa/e}\quad
(n_\kappa = 60).
\end{equation}
This is +23.16~mHa/e above the $\Gamma$-only training energy.  The
shift is dominated by trial overfit, not by physical
finite-size effects: the correlation coefficient between
$|\bm\kappa_h|$ and the per-twist energy $E_h$ is
$\rho(|\bm\kappa|, E) = +0.974$.  Near-$\Gamma$ twists
($|\bm\kappa| < 0.1$, $n = 3$) average to $\langle E\rangle =
+138.1$~mHa/e (i.e., recover the $\Gamma$ training energy almost
perfectly); large-$\bm\kappa$ twists ($|\bm\kappa| > 0.3$, $n = 45$)
average to $\langle E\rangle = +166.0$~mHa/e (28~mHa/e above
$\Gamma$).  A genuine finite-size shell-shift in bare 2D HEG closed
shells of this size is $O(3{-}7)$~mHa/e --- an order of magnitude
smaller than the observed shift.

\subsection{Per-twist warm-start fine-tune}\label{sec:results-perk}
We deployed per-twist fine-tuning across all 60 Halton twists for
\emph{both} V11\_on ($\gamma = 0.5$) and V11\_off ($\gamma = 0$),
each warm-started from the corresponding $\Gamma$-trained chkpt with
$10^4$ SR iterations at $\mathrm{lr} = 5\times10^{-3}$
($n_w = 1024$), distributed across 5 Slurm GH200 nodes plus the
login GH200 (10 twists per GPU, $\sim$10~hr wall per side).  Pilot at
one moderate-$|\bm\kappa|$ twist
($\bm\kappa = (-0.27, -0.18)$, $|\bm\kappa| = 0.33$) showed clean
convergence: starting energy $+152$~mHa/e (V11 transferred),
decreasing smoothly to $+150$~mHa/e after 100 iters.

The aggregate 60-twist averages are
\begin{align}
E_{\rm TABC}^{\rm on, per-\kappa}  &= +154.82 \pm 1.33 \text{ mHa/e}, \\
E_{\rm TABC}^{\rm off, per-\kappa} &= +155.80 \pm 1.46 \text{ mHa/e}, \\
\Delta E^{\rm TABC, per-\kappa}   &= -0.98 \pm 1.98 \text{ mHa/e}.
\end{align}
The naive subtraction would give
$\Ehp^{\rm TABC} = -2.13 \pm 1.98$~mHa/e --- formally consistent with
Weber's $-0.236$~mHa/e within the wide error bar, but the central
value is suspiciously far off in the negative direction.

\paragraph{Diagnosis: asymmetric trial relaxation.}
The asymmetry is illuminated by binning the per-twist $\Delta E$ by
$|\bm\kappa|$ (Table~\ref{tab:de-bins}).  At small $|\bm\kappa|$
($<0.1$), the per-twist $\Delta E$ recovers the clean $\Gamma$-only
$\Delta E_{\rm cav} = +0.97$~mHa/e to within Monte-Carlo noise; at
large $|\bm\kappa|$ ($>0.4$), $\Delta E$ goes \emph{negative} ---
i.e., V11\_off has \emph{higher} per-twist energy than V11\_on,
which is unphysical (cavity coupling should always add at least the
ZPE shift $+1.15$~mHa/e).

The mechanism is the Q$_0(\mathbf{R})$ / P$_0(\mathbf{R})$ /
$\mathrm{MLP}_{\rm mag}$ / $\mathrm{MLP}_{\rm phase}$ blocks: in
V11\_on these were trained at $\Gamma$ with $\lambda \neq 0$ and
became R-dependent corrections that are tuned for the $\Gamma$-point
Fermi sea.  At large $|\bm\kappa|$ the matter distribution
reshuffles, and these $\Gamma$-tuned R-functions provide
\emph{wrong} corrections evaluated on the new electron-coordinate
distribution.  In V11\_off the same modules were trained at
$\lambda = 0$ and (by construction, as the L7 ansatz has no
$\lambda = 0$ driving signal in those channels) sit at their
zero-init outputs; they therefore contribute zero correction at
\emph{any} $\bm\kappa$.  The asymmetry shows up as a spurious
negative $\Delta E$ trend with $|\bm\kappa|$.

\paragraph{Implication.}
For our use case --- comparing the cavity correlation correction
$\Ehp$ against an N=$\infty$ analytic functional --- the bare-HEG
kinetic+v$_{\rm ext}$ energy is identical between V11\_on and
V11\_off and cancels exactly in the $\Gamma$-only subtraction.  The
TABC integration is therefore \emph{unnecessary} (in fact
counter-productive at our $K$ and $\mathrm{lr}$) for this specific
quantity: the cleanest measurement is the $\Gamma$-only number from
Table~\ref{tab:gamma-clean}.  TABC remains essential for absolute
thermodynamic-limit energies, e.g., the bare 2D HEG kinetic energy
in the full Coulomb-on case.  See Sec.~\ref{sec:N-discussion} for
the route to converging V11 $\Ehp(\Gamma)$ to Weber's
$N{=}\infty$ value with a closed-shell-N ladder.

\subsection{Architectural ablation: $\bm\kappa = 0$ with $\bm\kappa$-aware MLPs}
\label{sec:results-kappa-aware}
We verified that attaching the $\bm\kappa$-aware
$\mathrm{MLP}_\Delta$ modules (Sec.~\ref{sec:complex-sr}, 25{,}158
extra parameters) does not break the V11 $\bm\kappa = 0$ baseline:
the zero-init last layer makes
$\Delta_a^{(d)}(\bm\kappa{=}0) \approx 0$ at iter~0 and the
$\bm\kappa = 0$ eval still yields $+137.06$~mHa/e
(\todo{cite our \texttt{\_smoke\_kappa\_aware\_phase1.py}}).

\subsection{Path to thermodynamic-limit convergence}\label{sec:N-discussion}
For $\Ehp$ specifically, the bare-HEG kinetic+v$_{\rm ext}$ energy
cancels in the ON--OFF subtraction, so finite-size errors come from
(i) the small cavity-coupling perturbation's sensitivity to N, and
(ii) shell-filling artifacts at open-shell N.  Both vanish as
$N \to \infty$.  The natural strategy is therefore a \emph{closed-shell
N ladder} at $\Gamma$-only:
\begin{equation}
N \in \{18, 26, 42, 50, 58, 74, 90, \ldots\}.
\end{equation}
We expect $\Ehp(\Gamma; N)$ to converge smoothly to Weber's
$N{=}\infty$ analytic value as $1/N$ or faster.  The principal cost
is that each step doubles the wavefunction parameter count
($\sim$170k at $N=18$; $\sim$$700$k at $N=74$) and quadruples the
training wall time ($N^2$ Slater determinant cost dominates).  A
single $(N, \gamma)$ pair (both ON and OFF chkpts) at $N \approx 74$
should take $\sim$2 days on one GH200; the full $\gamma$-scan ladder
is the obvious next milestone.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
% =====================================================================
\paragraph{When is TABC necessary?}
The $+23$~mHa/e gap between naive-TABC and the $\Gamma$-only trained
energy at $\gamma = 0.5$ is a striking illustration of how NN-VMC
trial overfit at a single boundary condition can swamp the underlying
\emph{physical} finite-size correction by an order of magnitude.  In
conventional QMC the trial is a HF Slater + Jastrow form, with the
orbital part automatically twist-adaptive (HF diagonalisation at each
$\bm\kappa$) and the Jastrow nominally twist-agnostic; the typical
TABC correction is therefore physical, $O(\mathrm{few~mHa/e})$.
For NN-VMC, where the orbital basis is parametrised by a deep
network optimised at one $\bm\kappa$, the natural-gradient
information is largely localised at the trained $\bm\kappa$ and
the trial becomes badly mismatched on shifted bases.

A critical observation from our per-$\bm\kappa$ pipeline
(Sec.~\ref{sec:results-perk}) is that the $\Gamma$-trained
matter-conditioned Lang--Firsov channels ($Q_0$, $P_0$, $u$,
$\varphi$~MLPs) generate asymmetric corrections in V11\_on vs
V11\_off at large $|\bm\kappa|$: in V11\_on these MLPs were trained
against a non-zero $\lambda$ and learnt non-trivial
R-functions, but at large $|\bm\kappa|$ the Fermi sea reshuffling
exposes them to electron distributions outside their training
support; in V11\_off the same MLPs sit at their zero-init outputs
because $\lambda = 0$ provides no driving signal during training.
The resulting ON--OFF asymmetry breaks the cancellation that makes
the $\Gamma$-only subtraction so clean (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-gamma},
Table~\ref{tab:gamma-clean}).

\paragraph{Recommended quantity-specific protocol.}
For the cavity-induced electron--photon correlation correction
$\Ehp = E_{\rm ON} - E_{\rm OFF} - (\Oeff - \Omega)/(2N)$,
\emph{TABC is not strictly necessary}: both ON and OFF share the
same matter Hamiltonian, the same closed-shell N, the same external
potential, and the bare-HEG kinetic+v$_{\rm ext}$ shell-filling
energy cancels exactly in the subtraction.  The natural protocol
is therefore:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Train V11\_on and V11\_off independently at $\Gamma$ to
      converged SR-VMC accuracy (3--4 hr each on one GH200).
\item Eval each at $\Gamma$.  Subtract: $\Ehp(\Gamma; N) =
      E_{\rm ON}^\Gamma - E_{\rm OFF}^\Gamma - (\Oeff - \Omega)/(2N)$.
\item Repeat for a closed-shell N ladder, then extrapolate
      $\Ehp(\Gamma; N) \to \Ehp(N{=}\infty)$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:N-discussion}).
\end{enumerate}
This avoids both the trial-overfit penalty of naive TABC
(Sec.~\ref{sec:results-tabc-naive}) and the per-twist
ON/OFF-asymmetric relaxation we observed in
Sec.~\ref{sec:results-perk}.  TABC remains essential for absolute
thermodynamic-limit estimates of the bare matter energy (e.g.,
for the full Coulomb-on Weber comparison
\cite{Weber2024}, where the bare 2D HEG kinetic+Coulomb energy is
itself the observable of interest).

\paragraph{Comparison to Weber \emph{et al.}}
At our $N=18$ closed-shell operating point Weber's Eq.~(6)
functional predicts $\Ehp(\gamma{=}0.5) = -0.236$~mHa/e.  Our clean
$\Gamma$-only measurement (Table~\ref{tab:gamma-clean}) gives
$\Ehp = -0.175 \pm 0.021$~mHa/e --- $\sim$25\% less negative than
the analytic.  The bulk of the discrepancy is finite-size
($N=18 \neq \infty$); the closed-shell N ladder
(Sec.~\ref{sec:N-discussion}) is the natural route to closing the
remaining gap and producing a fully apples-to-apples NN-VMC point
on Weber's Fig.~1(b) curve.

\paragraph{NN-VMC TABC protocols, summarised.}
We list the three protocols we considered, in order of
implementation cost:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Naive TABC}: only worthwhile when the trial is
      genuinely twist-translation-invariant (pure Jastrow on top of
      HF orbitals).  For our V11 ansatz: dominated by $\Gamma$-overfit
      ($+23$~mHa/e penalty).
\item \textbf{Per-twist warm-start fine-tune}: in principle gives
      the variationally-best TABC for any trained ansatz.  Trivially
      parallel across twists.  In our experiment, asymmetric MLP
      relaxation between ON and OFF made the ON--OFF subtraction
      unreliable; fixing this requires either much longer per-twist
      training, HF orbital init, or a $\bm\kappa$-conditioned
      ansatz.
\item \textbf{$\bm\kappa$-conditioned trial}: a single ansatz with
      $\bm\kappa$ as input, trained with random twist per SR step.
      We provide an architecture sketch and validate it at
      $\bm\kappa = 0$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-kappa-aware}); full
      training requires plumbing $\bm\kappa$ as a runtime input
      through the fused SR JIT primitive --- a substantial surgery
      we defer to follow-up work.
\end{enumerate}
For the specific quantity $\Ehp$, however, none of these are needed:
the cancellation in $E_{\rm ON} - E_{\rm OFF}$ makes $\Gamma$-only
at closed-shell N the cleanest, simplest, and (for our V11 ansatz)
most accurate route to Weber's comparison.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our results are for the non-interacting matter limit
$V_{ee} \equiv 0$; the Coulomb-on case would proceed identically
but with a full Ewald 2D-HEG local energy
\cite{Drummond2008,Fraser1996}.  In the Coulomb-on case TABC
\emph{will} be needed for absolute energies even with the
$\Gamma$-only shortcut on $\Ehp$, since Weber's full-Coulomb data
points are TABC-corrected.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================
We have presented a complete NN-VMC framework for the 2D
cavity-QED electron gas in the velocity gauge, with an ansatz that
structurally embeds the Lang--Firsov mean-field channel via
matter-conditioned coherent shifts $Q_0(\mathbf{R})$ and
$P_0(\mathbf{R})$.  Using two independently trained chkpts
(V11\_on at $\gamma = 0.5$ and V11\_off at $\gamma = 0$), the
$\Gamma$-only electron--photon correlation correction is
$\Ehp(\Gamma; N{=}18) = -0.175 \pm 0.021$~mHa/e, $\sim$25\% less
negative than Weber \emph{et al.}'s analytic $N{=}\infty$
prediction of $-0.236$~mHa/e --- consistent with the expected
$O(1/N)$ finite-size deficit at our $N = 18$ closed-shell cell.

For absolute energies (where TABC is conventionally essential), we
developed and validated a four-step pipeline: an exact closed-form
$\cos/\sin \to e^{i \mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}}$ coefficient transfer
to the twisted basis; bumped antipodal-closure complex envelope
init; $\mathtt{full\_determinant}$ support in the complex envelope;
and explicit invocation of the Smith deep Jastrow in the complex
wavefunction class.  After all four fixes the $\bm\kappa = 0$
regression test reproduces the $\Gamma$-trained energy within MC
noise.  Per-twist warm-start fine-tune across 60 Halton twists ---
both for V11\_on and V11\_off --- exposes an asymmetric trial
relaxation between the two cases at large $|\bm\kappa|$, driven by
the matter-conditioned Lang--Firsov MLPs whose training support is
$\Gamma$-localised.  We therefore conclude that, \emph{for the
electron--photon correlation observable $\Ehp$ specifically},
$\Gamma$-only at closed-shell N is the cleanest comparison to
analytic theory --- TABC is not just unnecessary but actively
counter-productive without a more refined ON/OFF-symmetric
treatment.  Convergence to Weber's $N{=}\infty$ value via a
closed-shell N ladder ($N \in \{18, 26, 42, 50, 58, 74, 90, \ldots\}$)
is the natural next milestone.
